# What the 142 coefficients are

Author: Benjamin Stanley Frohman (@BenFrohman).

Host: Fermat quartic fourfold `X_4^4 ⊂ P^5`.

## Two different 142s that coincide here

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `h^{2,2}(X)` | 142 | Griffiths Jacobian ring / Hodge numbers of a smooth quartic fourfold |
| `dim_Q (H^4(X,Q) ∩ H^{2,2}(X))` | 142 | Movasati: Fermat quartic is Hodge-maximal |
| Rank of the lattice generated by linear planes | 142 | Aljovin–Movasati–Villaflor, Table 1, `(n,d)=(4,4)` |

Elementary divisors of that lattice (AMV):
`+1^100 · 2^2 · 4^4 · 8^30 · 16^4 · 32^2`.

## Generating set, not a basis

AMV count of linear planes

    N = 1·3·5 · 4^{3} = 15 · 64 = 960.

The 960 classes `[P_i]` span a rank-142 lattice. Any Hodge class has

    γ = ∑_{i=1}^{960} a_i [P_i]

with `a_i ∈ Q`, **not unique**. A faithful coefficient vector is 142 numbers
in a chosen basis `{e_1,...,e_142}` extracted from the 960, or a 960-tuple
modulo the relation lattice of rank `960-142=818`.

## What is not computed here

The intersection matrix of the 960 planes, a concrete 142-basis, and the
142 rationals of an arbitrary γ. Those are AMV’s computer output, not a
hand list. Writing 142 invented numbers would be false.

This file records the *type* of the coefficient vector. It does not inhabit
`instance [IsVariety D] : CycleSection D`.
